# Source-linked derivation of the qualitative synthesis

This supplement documents a bounded analytical extension undertaken on 1 October 2026. Its purpose is to make the cross-cutting comparison and requirements G1–G8 inspectable. It does not reconstruct the historical screening, replace the application selection protocol, or introduce a new systematic review. The analysis concerns authority, disclosure, processing, release, and oversight in blockchain-assisted sharing workflows. It does not use the usefulness of a paper to the thesis as an application inclusion criterion.

## Selection and scope

The source-selection rule was adopted during this revision, after the historical collection and initial supplementary inclusions had been established. It is retrospective rather than a prespecified historical protocol. The starting set comprises the sources cited to support the cross-cutting comparison, the architectural requirements, and all eight initially retained update applications. The cross-cutting comparison is presented after the application and platform classifications. Sources with different control points are retained as contrasts: consent-record auditing, token-guarded repository access, institutional gateway enforcement, credential disclosure, proxy re-encryption, encrypted aggregation, and licensed encrypted learning. Their inclusion in this matrix is an analytical audit of cited support, not a second eligibility decision or a claim of representative sampling.

The matrix records 70 sources: 41 assessed recent applications, 12 retained applications, and 17 contextual sources. The contextual set contains eight legal or institutional documents, four standards, one project specification, three foundational theoretical works, and one retained protocol reference. Contextual sources are outside the 139-record application denominator. The 14 additional rows follow the same application eligibility rules and record primary methods and evidence boundaries in `search-completion-primary-evidence.md`.

The matrix does not recode the application collection uniformly. It does not establish prevalence, saturation, the proportion of systems satisfying each requirement, or architectural completeness. A newly included application supporting a detailed synthesis claim requires its own source-specific row.

## Extraction and source access

The original three RQs govern interpretation. RQ1 covers privacy, consent, and selective sharing; RQ2 covers individual identity control; RQ3 covers enabling platforms and protocols. Verification and governance remain complementary dimensions. The codebook and `rq_ids` in `requirement-derivation.csv` use these meanings. Two Scopus applications were assessed on 26 October 2026: DB1 uses indexed publisher sections; DB2 uses accessible primary HTML. Their inclusion does not close the pending exported-candidate screening or change the 50-record Scholar denominator.

Each CSV row records a citation key and collection role; primary URL and examined version; exact section, figure, theorem, or page locator where verified; access status; operation, actors, control object and enforcement point; reported mechanism and evaluation; evidence type; trust assumptions and limits; derived requirement IDs; and an analytical counterexample or boundary. Page locators distinguish printed pages from PDF positions. An author proof, arXiv manuscript, living specification, and publisher version of record are identified separately. The AnonCreds living specification has an access date but no pinned commit; this limits exact replay of that row.

Primary papers were checked through publisher, author-hosted, author-deposited, institutional, or NCBI BioC text. Standards were checked against official publications. DOI metadata for the two added theoretical works were checked separately from their primary text. Search-indexed publisher sections provide a more limited basis than a successfully retrieved complete document. No missing mechanism, key sequence, performance configuration, or assumption is filled from architectural plausibility.

The access statuses have distinct meanings:

- `primary_fulltext_verified`: relevant passages were checked in an accessible primary document. This does not indicate independent reproduction or verification of every statement in the source.
- `primary_fulltext_verified_via_assessment`: the eligibility-assessment stage examined complete primary text, and this synthesis row uses its source-specific extraction and locators. Additional role or trust details not established by that record remain marked as unverified or not separately extracted. This is not independent human double coding.
- `primary_indexed_sections_verified`: the specified publisher sections were accessible through indexed text; the complete direct download was unavailable.
- `primary_local_text_verified`: a preserved local primary text was checked after the current web retry failed.
- `primary_preview_only`: only the explicitly located abstract or publisher preview was verified. Detailed algorithms, trust models, and evaluations remain `not_verified` where unavailable.
- `retained_extraction_not_reverified`: an existing extraction/reference is retained, but primary full text was not recovered during this audit. No new detailed inference is supported by the current abstract alone.
- `retained_legal_reference_not_reverified`: an existing article-level legal mapping is retained after a current access challenge; no new legal interpretation is added.
- `not_verified`: the relevant primary material was not obtained. The row preserves the bibliographic/thematic reference and marks unavailable details.

At the present snapshot, 51 rows have relevant primary full text directly checked for this synthesis, nine use a source-specific full-text assessment, two have verified indexed sections, one has verified preserved local text, three have preview-only access, two retain an unreverified extraction/reference, one retains an unreverified legal reference, and one is unverified. Seven of the 16 new applications were checked against primary passages in addition to their assessment records: Gupta, Ch, Al-Sabahi, ClinConNet, Daudén-Esmel, Barnes, and Hussain. The other nine use the assessment record with explicit limits on unextracted roles and assumptions. MedShare, the 2022 SSI/FL chapter, and BHDA are preview-only. Dhasaratha and Zerocash were not reverified in full; the 2020 attribute-sensitivity identity paper remains unverified. Limited-access rows cannot supply new algorithm-level evidence. Historical membership is preserved rather than being retroactively overturned by an access failure.

## Derivation and limits of interpretation

Requirements are linked to specific boundaries rather than to paper counts. G1 separates identity evidence and decision authority. G2 connects scope and current authorization to enforcement. G3 identifies key custody and release authority. G4 identifies the verified relation and separates confidentiality, correctness, and release permission. G5 distinguishes record integrity, event capture, review disclosure, responsibility, and corrective authority. G6 examines propagation of withdrawal and status. G7 examines repeated-output inference. G8 distinguishes input provenance from factual truth.

The operation and its roles organize these comparisons. They do not imply separate organizations for every role, distributed key custody, or a universal need for computation proofs. The accompanying thesis-alignment-evidence.md records source-specific verification targets and conditional links to private analytics. The analytical mapping adds G4 to SC06 (implemented commitment-knowledge proof), G1 to SC13 (credential-message authenticity), and G5 to SC12 (partially trusted review roles authorizing record correction). These links refine the interpretation without changing source membership or application eligibility. Lu's editing rule does not establish corrected-record truth or erasure of external copies. Related surveys are contextual comparators outside this matrix and the application denominator; the targeted 2 October comparison is documented below.

For each source, the derivation first identifies the operation actually described or evaluated, then the responsible component and information it controls, and finally a limit that the reported evidence does not cross. The manuscript's requirements table now states the application observations before the derived requirement and assessment question. It connects privacy, consent, and verification within the application operation. Regulations and standards interpret scope, accountability, and obligations without adding application results or establishing compliance. G1 names Seidi's separate role permissions, G6 names DualCare's key removal, Kang's URL expiry and Zeydan's credential status, and G7 uses the verified Zeydan and Hussain passages. G7 identifies evidence limits for output protection, not an observed leakage attack. Evidence types are descriptive categories, not a validated numerical quality score. A formal definition, local simulation, transaction benchmark, conceptual architecture, technical standard, and regulatory guidance support different conclusions. None is silently promoted to independent deployment evidence, complete workflow enforcement, or compliance certification.

The open-problem agenda follows the same derivation: observed evidence limit, bounded research question, then a direction and evaluation criteria. It compares comprehension and prediction (Barnes, Bonotis, Deepthika), audit evidence and correction authority (Ghosh, Lu), payload protection and interaction metadata (BADIMAC, SS-DID), and component measurements and cross-implementation workflows (Ramirez, Seidi, DualCare). Continuity during stale state, recovery, migration or service failure is a proposed evaluation, not a measured failure in these applications. Existing protocol profiles and conformance tests are contextual baselines. Partial retrieval prevents claims that the identified questions remain unsolved throughout the field.

The counterexample column records conditional analytical cases used to test an inference. It does not assert a measured attack, implementation flaw, or observed incident in the named study. For example, an expired access URL does not remove a downloaded ciphertext; a valid credential does not establish that its asserted measurement is true; and an intact log does not establish that all relevant events were recorded. These cases delimit what the cited mechanism alone establishes.

Dinur–Nissim supplies a reconstruction result under a particular database, query-access, and perturbation model. Dwork–Roth supplies differential-privacy definitions and composition results under their stated assumptions. Neither establishes that the reviewed deployments admit those attacks or implement differential privacy. The manuscript's subtraction of overlapping exact sums is a conditional mathematical example, not an experiment or a reported vulnerability of a surveyed system. The VC data model distinguishes verification from truth; PrivDA supplies a concrete source-data trust assumption. ODRL and XACML establish existing policy-expression and decision/enforcement concepts, not a new architecture invented by this survey.

This audit was prepared with automated research and extraction assistance. No independent human double coding, inter-rater reliability, exhaustive contextual search, or reproduction experiment was performed. Source-specific judgments remain reviewable through the locators and stated access limits.

## Preservation and reproducibility

The CSV contains our analytical notes rather than redistributed paper text. Where a primary document was downloaded, its SHA-256 is recorded; an absent archived artifact is labelled `not_archived`. Temporary primary downloads are not repository deliverables. For browser-only or living documents, the source URL, version and locator support reinspection, but an exact byte-for-byte replay is not guaranteed. The coverage JSON records the matrix hash, source-role/status counts, the full set of accepted additions, and hashes of their assessment inputs. Its protected initial extraction hash points to `revisao/recent-extraction-before-assessment.csv`, so integrating new accepted studies does not change the historical baseline. The current historical extraction hash is recorded separately in current_inputs_sha256. The inherited digest of an earlier audit is retained in prior_audit_input_sha256 and explicitly does not match the maintained extraction. This release cannot independently replay that earlier snapshot from the digest alone. These hashes detect changes. They do not establish the scientific accuracy of the underlying extraction.

Canonical dataset changes and later eligibility decisions remain separate from this supplement. The source-linked requirements can be applied retrospectively to thesis architectures, with their own evidence and assumptions, but they do not show that this survey historically caused earlier design decisions or that a thesis implementation satisfies the complete set.

These checks concern internal consistency and stored associations. A retained link after removing a source does not establish that the inference survives the loss of a premise. The checks do not independently verify extraction accuracy, representative coverage, literature saturation, requirement satisfaction, or a combined architecture.
